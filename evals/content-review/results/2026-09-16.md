## Notes

- **The headline result:** `add-topic`'s Stage 3 review, as currently
  worded, does what it's supposed to do. All four genuinely planted
  violations (undefined jargon, AI-patterned tone, over-explained
  figurative language, an unverified technical claim) were caught,
  correctly categorized, and in CR-01's case the review went further
  than the plant itself — independently web-searching and debunking two
  factual claims in the fixture that weren't deliberately planted as
  wrong (I hadn't verified the Redis/Chrome Safe Browsing examples
  myself when writing the fixture). That's a stronger result than the
  eval was designed to test for: it demonstrates the review does real
  independent verification rather than pattern-matching against a known
  list of tells.
- **CR-05 is a genuine finding about scenario design, not about the
  review.** The intent was a "nothing to flag" control. The review's
  actual findings (no concrete/numeric example; "container",
  "orchestrator", "Kubernetes" used without a defining clause) are
  defensible against the literal text of the Writing Standard — they
  are not invented nitpicks the way the grading criteria in
  `HOW_TO_RUN.md` describes a FAIL. This means the CR-05 draft, despite
  being written carefully as a "clean" baseline, still had two real
  gaps I didn't notice while writing it — which is itself a small,
  ironic demonstration of exactly why this repo's review steps use a
  second, fresh set of eyes rather than a self-check. **Action for a
  future run:** either tighten CR-05's draft further (add a one-line
  concrete example and define "container"/"orchestrator" inline) so it
  actually clears every Writing Standard bullet, or reframe CR-05's
  Expected finding as "no violation of the four planted-violation
  categories (jargon/tone/figurative/correctness), cosmetic gaps
  aside" rather than a literal zero-finding bar — the latter may be a
  more realistic definition of "clean" for a thorough reviewer, since a
  review that never finds a single polish nit on any draft is arguably
  under-scrutinizing, not over-passing.
- No scenario came back with a finding that was flatly wrong or
  fabricated — even CR-05's "excess" findings were real, checkable
  gaps against the actual standard text, not hallucinations. That's the
  more important signal from this run than the literal PASS count.
