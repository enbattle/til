## Notes

- SR-05 is the most valuable result in this batch: it's the one scenario
  designed to test _process_ (investigate before committing to a route)
  rather than just the final answer, and the agent actually did that
  unprompted — it wasn't told to investigate first, it chose to, then
  reported that it had. Worth watching this one specifically in future
  runs since it's easiest for a routing rule change to break without
  anyone noticing (an instruction change could make a session commit to
  `/feature` or a direct fix _before_ looking at the code, and still
  land on the "right" label by luck).
- SR-04's phrasing was slightly garbled in the raw agent response (a
  run-on sentence mentioning "the add-topic skill's own writing-standard
  review approach informally isn't even required") but the underlying
  decision and reasoning were correct — noted here rather than silently
  cleaned up, since a consistently garbled explanation across runs would
  itself be worth investigating.
- No FAILs to analyze this round. That's a reasonable outcome for a
  first run graded against scenarios written with the current
  `CLAUDE.md`/`SKILL.md` wording already in mind — it mainly confirms
  the eval harness itself works end-to-end (fresh agents, no
  contamination from the orchestrating session, distinguishable
  PASS/FAIL grading) rather than proving the routing rules are robust
  against future wording drift. The real test of that comes from re-runs
  after future edits to `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/SDLC.md`, or a `SKILL.md`.
